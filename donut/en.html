    What is DonutTimer?

    DonutTimer is a free online timer that shows the time left as a donut. As the minutes pass, the donut is eaten clockwise from 12 o'clock, one small bite at a time; when it's gone, time is up. You can see how much is left at a glance, without staring at numbers. Under the frosting it's the same engine as TimerTrain, a timer that plays several timers in a row.

    How to use it

    1) Press "Add timer" to line up as many timers as you need — work, break, a warm-up. 2) Set each one's length, name and flavor. 3) Choose how many rounds and press play. A sound marks every switch, and the next donut takes over automatically. Copy the share link to send your exact setup to someone else.

    Six flavors

    Strawberry milk, chocolate, mint chocolate, vanilla, blueberry and milk tea. Each timer can have its own flavor, so "strawberry for work, blueberry for breaks" tells you which part you're in even from across the room.

    Good for

    Pomodoro sessions, study blocks, chores, workout intervals, or a calm "five more minutes" signal at home. Leave it open on a tablet and the shrinking donut does the reminding.

    A cute, aesthetic timer you will enjoy looking at

    DonutTimer is designed to be nice to look at: pastel icing, colorful sprinkles and rounded type, in light or dark mode. It is the kind of aesthetic study timer you can leave open on your desk or share on screen during a study-with-me session.

    A visual timer for kids and classrooms

    Because the time left is shown as how much donut is left, children who cannot read a clock yet can still see how much longer. Teachers can project it for clean-up time, tests or group work, and parents can use it for brushing teeth or getting ready in the morning.

    FAQ

    Is DonutTimer free?
    Yes. No sign-up and no install — open it in any browser on a phone, tablet or computer.

    How is it different from TimerTrain?
    Same features, different look: TimerTrain shows time as a ring, DonutTimer as a donut. The button at the top right switches between them and keeps your setup.

    Can people who can't read a clock use it?
    The amount of donut left shows how much time remains, so it works even for people who find a clock face or numbers hard to read.

    Does it make a sound?
    Yes — when one timer switches to the next and when everything is finished. Pick the sound and volume in the settings.

    Is there a cute, aesthetic Pomodoro timer?
    Yes. DonutTimer is a free, aesthetic online timer: pastel icing, colorful sprinkles and rounded type, with the time left shown as a donut. Its Pomodoro page runs 25 minutes of focus and 5 minutes of break on repeat, in any browser with no sign-up.

    Can I use it as a visual timer for kids or in a classroom?
    Yes. The amount of donut left shows how much time remains, so children who cannot read a clock yet can still see how much longer. It works well for clean-up time, brushing teeth, getting ready in the morning, tests and group work, and it is easy to read when projected on a big screen.
